How it works

Fleet: every airliner registered to Air Canada, Air Canada rouge LP or Jazz Aviation LP in Transport Canada's civil aircraft register, refreshed weekly. The register counts aircraft that are stored or awaiting retirement, so a few old ones will show as not seen.

Positions: ADS-B signals picked up by volunteer receivers of the adsb.lol network (open data, ODbL), collected every ~5 minutes. Over oceans and remote areas there are no receivers, so a plane can be flying but out of range.

Parked: the airport where the plane was last seen on the ground or on final approach. Maintenance is inferred, since no public source reports it: parked for 2+ days, sitting at a known maintenance or storage site, or not seen at all for 3+ days. Routes come from two crowd-sourced databases (adsb.lol and adsbdb) and are only shown when the plane's position and heading actually match them; when they don't, the page says the route is unknown rather than guessing.

The aircraft drawings are generic profiles I made for this page; they aren't official Air Canada artwork. This is a personal project and isn't affiliated with Air Canada.
