Commercial Airline Program

Three disciplines run the commercial side of an airline, and they never stop talking to each other. Network Planning decides where to fly and with how much capacity. Revenue Management decides how to price and allocate that capacity. Loyalty decides how to keep the people who already fly with you coming back, and how much of that same capacity to give away as rewards. I've worked inside two of the three so far, first in Revenue Management and now in Loyalty at Air Canada, and this page is where I'm building a short course on each, plus a map of how they fit together.

How the Courses Connect

Network Planning
decides where to fly, how often, and with which aircraft
→ sets the fixed capacity RM has to work with
Revenue Management
decides who gets that capacity, and at what price
→ prices award seats like any other fare class
Loyalty
decides who redeems that capacity for free, and who gets recognized
It runs the other way too
A redemption isn't free to the airline — RM has to put a bid price on an award seat just like it would a discount fare, which is the same opportunity-cost logic RM 101 covers for cash fares. And a strong elite base is itself a network-planning input: it's part of the case for defending or growing a route long after the raw economics stop looking obvious on their own.
