About Daily Turkmen

A personal project by Grégoire Maillard, the sister course of Daily Turkish.

The course

Daily Turkmen teaches standard written Turkmen, in the Latin alphabet used in Turkmenistan today, to English speakers starting from zero: you need no other language to follow it. It is built like Daily Turkish: levels, units and short lessons, a grammar guide and a culture interlude in every unit, and a flashcard deck.

There is no audio. Browsers have no Turkmen voice, and a voice for another language would mispronounce it, so the guides describe each sound with comparisons from English instead.

Beta

The course is new. Its Turkmen was written with AI assistance and checked against dictionaries where they could be reached; a native speaker reviews each level, and the beta label comes off once they have. Until then, expect the odd mistake.

The method

Anki and spaced repetition. Anki is the free flashcard app that made spaced repetition popular. After each card you grade how well you remembered it (Again, Hard, Good or Easy), and it comes back just before you would forget it: tomorrow, then in a few days, then weeks and months later. A few minutes a day are enough to keep thousands of words. The flashcards here use Anki's own algorithm (SM-2) with its default settings, the same four grades with the next interval shown on each button, and Anki's statistics (a card is “mature” after 21 days).

Fluent Forever. In his book Fluent Forever (2014), Gabriel Wyner argues for learning pronunciation first, the most useful words before the rare ones, words in real sentences rather than lists, recalling actively instead of just recognising, and letting spaced repetition do the remembering. Daily Turkmen follows this as far as it can without audio: the course opens with the letters and their sounds; the deck is 1,000 useful words, the most useful first; every card has an example sentence; and words are practised in both directions, English → Turkmen once you recognise the Turkmen.
