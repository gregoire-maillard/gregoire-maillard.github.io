# Turkmen A1 → B1 · Project spec

The rules for the Turkmen course (working name **Daily Turkmen**), a sister of the Turkish course and built from its code. Every conversation that works on it reads this file in full first, then the parts of [TM-CURRICULUM.md](TM-CURRICULUM.md) it needs, then the sections of [turkish/PROJECT.md](../turkish/PROJECT.md) this file points to, and finishes by adding a line to the progress log at the bottom. [TM-PROMPTS.md](TM-PROMPTS.md) has the opening prompt of each conversation.

**How this file relates to the Turkish spec.** The Turkmen course reuses the Turkish course's design: the same pages, data schemas, exercise types, answer checker, SRS scheduler, progress store and validator, forked into Turkmen files. Where this file says nothing, the Turkish spec applies **with the renames of section 5.0**. Where they differ, this file wins. When the Turkish spec changes later, the Turkmen course does not follow automatically: a conversation that wants a Turkish improvement ports it and logs it here.

**Contents:** 1 Goals · 2 Fixed decisions · 3 Files and pages · 4 IDs · 5 Data schemas · 6 Runtime behaviour · 7 Content style rules · 8 Validation · 9 Workflow for a conversation · 10 Progress log

## 1. Goals

- A Turkmen course from A1 to B1 for English speakers, in the spirit of Daily Turkish: a path of units and short lessons, many small exercises, immediate feedback, XP and a streak.
- **For English speakers starting from zero.** No knowledge of Turkish (or any other Turkic language) is assumed. **Lessons are never built as a bridge from Turkish**: lessons, hints, feedback, guides, interludes and flashcards explain Turkmen on its own terms, in English, and every explanation must work for someone who has never heard Turkish. A short factual remark that Turkmen and Turkish are related or alike is allowed where it is interesting (*Turkmen sentence structure works like Turkish: the verb comes last*), at most once in a guide or interlude, never in lessons, hints or feedback, and never as the way a point is taught (no Turkish forms to translate from, no "where Turkish has X, Turkmen has Y").
- **Structure and format may follow Daily Turkish, and say so.** The course deliberately mirrors Daily Turkish in structure (levels, units, lessons, exercise types, guides, interludes, flashcards), layout and formatting, and the pages may present it as Daily Turkish's sister (the shared look, the link between the two sites, "from the makers of Daily Turkish"). Writers and build conversations compare with the Turkish course freely: its code, schemas, files and formats are the template, and the notes for writers in TM-CURRICULUM.md compare the two languages to say what the template gets wrong for Turkmen.
- Every point of Turkmen grammar up to B1 (scope in TM-CURRICULUM.md), explained in a grammar guide per unit and practised in that unit's lessons.
- One culture or history interlude per unit, about Turkmenistan and the Turkmen, factual and neutral.
- A deck of 1,000 useful Turkmen words, reviewed with Anki-style spaced repetition.
- Correct content: standard written Turkmen as in the official orthographic dictionary, accepted-answer lists that are fair to the learner, and a native speaker's review of every batch before the beta label comes off (section 9).

**Not in scope for now:** B2 to C2, audio of any kind, accounts and sync (local progress with export and import, as the Turkish course had before ACCT-1), the iOS app, any content copied from other courses or textbooks.

### Morphology engine (`assets/js/turkmen-morph.js`)

A rule-based model of how written Turkmen builds its words (TK-MORPH), built like the Turkish engine (turkish/PROJECT.md 3) but not loading it. The validator checks every `conjugate` and `morph` exercise and the forms on the flashcards against it (section 8), and `scripts/turkmen/morph-test.mjs` tests it on forms typed by hand. It is a classic script like `turkmen.js`: in a page it exposes `window.TKMMorph` (and `TKM.morph` when it loads after `turkmen.js`); in Node, `require()` returns the same object. No page loads it yet; one that needs it adds `<script defer src="/assets/js/turkmen-morph.js"></script>` after `turkmen.js`. No dependencies. Scope: every form A1 to B1 teach (B1 added in QA-TK-1).

**What it knows.** Suffixes are written in the notation of TM-CURRICULUM.md (A = a/e, Y = y/i, (Y) and (A) linking vowels after a consonant, (n) and (s) buffer letters after a vowel), plus Ä = a/ä for the suffixes whose front form has ä (-ýÄr *gelýär*, -rÄk *kiçiräk*), and attached one at a time, with:

- front/back harmony on the last vowel (ä is front), and the **rounding rule** of TM-CURRICULUM.md: the vowel of the second syllable rounds after a rounded first syllable unless it ends the word (*gözüm, öýüne, boldum, gowurak*, but *gözi, boldy*), y/i from the third syllable on (*öýümiz, bolupdyr*); -mY counts as part of the word (*gowumy*) but its own vowel never rounds (*ýokmydy*);
- voicing of a noun's final k, p, ç before a vowel when it has two or more syllables (*kitaby, çöregi, agajy*), of t only from a list (*Aşgabady*, but *döwleti*), and of one-syllable words only when their vowel is long, from a list with the homographs told apart by sense (*at* "name" → *adym*, *at* "horse" → *atym*); the closed list of verbs that voice t to d before a vowel and before -ýAr (*gidýär, gider, gidip*, but *gitdi, gitjek*);
- vowel loss from a list (*ogly, burnum, agzy*); the dative fused with a final vowel (*Mara, okuwça, ejä, kişä, oba*); -ny and -nyň after a vowel (*Aýnany, Aýnanyň*); the pronominal n after a third-person possessive and after -kY (*öýüne, kitabynda*); the pronouns and demonstratives written out (*maňa, oňa, muny, şonuň*);
- **e → ä**: a final e before the dative, -ny, -nyň, the possessives -(Y)m, -(Y)ň, -(Y)mYz, -(Y)ňYz and -kY (*ejä, nämäni, hepdäniň, ejäm, öýdäki*), and an e-final verb stem or the negative -mA before -(Y)p, -(Y)ň, -An, -Ar and the optative (*işläp, işlär, gelmäň, işläýin*);
- "to be": the zero form first, -dYr with the personal endings, the formal written form (*talypdyryn, talypdyrsyň, talypdyr*), and last the bare endings (*talybyn*), *däl* with the same endings, *bar* and *ýok*, the past -dY (*talypdym, öýdedik, bardy*), the question particle written attached (*talypmy, talypmydyň*);
- verbs: -ýAr, -dY, -jAk and -mAlY without endings, -Ar with -mAr and -mAz, the imperatives and optatives, -sYn, -(Y)p, -(Y)p bilmek in three tenses, -(Y)p with *otyr, dur, ýatyr, ýör*, the present of the four posture verbs on their own (*otyryn, durun, ýatyrsyň, ýörüs*; A2-4), -(Y)pdYr in the third person, -An with the locative (*gelende, işlände, gelenimde*); compound verbs change only their last word (*pikir edýär*);
- word building: -lY, -sYz, -çY, -lYk (*k* → *g* before a vowel: *dostlugy*), -çA, -rAk, which voices a final k, t, p or ç of the stem (*agrak, gögräk, oňadrak*; A2-4), and the diminutives -jYk and -jAgAz (*kiçijik, oglanjyk, gyzjagaz*; A2-4);
- **B1 (QA-TK-1)**: the compound tenses, the past copula -dY after -ýAr, -(Y)p, -Ar, -jAk and -mAlY with the past endings (*gelýärdim, gelipdim, gelerdim, geljekdim, gelmelidim*), negatives *gelmeýärdim, gelmändim, gelmezdim, geljek däldim* (and *gelmejekdim*), *gelmeli däldim* (and *gelmemelidim*), and the question particle **before** -dY, as with nouns (*bilýärmidiň?*); the past progressive (*okap otyrdym*); the full -(Y)pdYr, with no -dYr in the second persons (*gelipdirin, gelipsiň, gelipdir*; negative *gelmändirin, gelmändirsiň*; *gelipmisiň?*; open question 6, settled here); -(Y)p bilerdi and bilmezdi; the conditional -sA with the past endings (*gelsem … gelseler*, *gelmesem*) and *bolsa* after a participle or a noun (*gelýän bolsam, gelen bolsam, geljek bolsam, öýde bolsam*); *eken* after a participle, with the past endings after a consonant for I and you (*almaly ekenim, bermeli ekeniň*); -dYr for a guess (*gelendir, edendir*); the participles -ýAn, -An, -jAk and their negatives (*gelmeýän, gelmedik, gelmejek*), -mAdYk; the verbal noun -mAk with cases and possessives, its dative -mAgA with *ä* in front (*okamaga, gelmäge, işletmäge*), -(Y)ş (*geliş, okaýyş*); -dYgY on a participle or a noun (*gelendigini, gitjekdigimi, kyndygyny*); the converbs -Andan, -mAzdAn, -mAn, -ýAnçA with the past endings (*gelýänçäm*; *gelýänçä* or *gelýänçe*), -kA (*çagakam, gelýärkäm*, which A2 lacked), -(A)lY, -A (*gide, güle*); the voices, as verb stems that conjugate like any verb: passive -Yl, -l after a vowel, -Yn after *l*, -n after *l* and a vowel (*ýazyl-, okal-, gelin-, arassalan-*; the last since B1-4), reflexive -(Y)n, reciprocal -(Y)ş, causative -t after a vowel and, since B1-5, after a stem of two or more syllables ending in *l* or *r* (*okat-, azalt-, bejert-*), -dYr after any other consonant, and the irregular causatives of `LEX.causative` (*görkez-, getir-, turuz-, duruz-*); the causative -t voices before a vowel and before -ýAr, as every verb stem of two or more syllables ending in t now does (*okadýar, okadyp*, but *okatdy*; B1-5); verbs from nouns -lA, -lAş, -lAn, -Al, which is -l after *a* or *e* and drops a final *y* or *i* (*arassala-, gowulaş-, öýlen-, azal-*; *gysgal-, ulal-, kiçel-* since B1-5). TM-CURRICULUM.md (open question 6) says which forms rest on a source and which on the rule alone.

The word lists are `TKMMorph.LEX`: long-vowel monosyllables and homographs, nouns in -t that voice, words that keep their consonant, vowel loss (in nouns, and since FC-4 in the verb stems of `verbDrop`, before a vowel only: *doglar, doglup*), the verbs that voice t (since B1-5 a stem of two or more syllables ending in t voices by rule, so the list matters for one-syllable stems), the irregular causatives, one-syllable loanwords whose rounding is unsure. When the validator flags a correct form, the fix usually belongs there, with a test. TM-CURRICULUM.md ("Open questions for native review", 1–5 and 9) says what each rule rests on and which points are open.

**Other calls.** `conjugate(base, { tense, neg, q, p, ability, aux })` and `copula(pred, { tense, neg, q, p })` build any of these forms; `decline(noun, { pl, poss, case, ky, sense })` declines a noun (*kitaplarymda*); `converb(verb)` gives -(Y)p; `build(base, ["LOC", "KY"])` applies suffixes by id (`TKMMorph.SUFFIXES`); `analyze(base, tiles)` says whether morph tiles are the base and its suffixes cut at morpheme boundaries, and if not, what the same suffixes build (*mekdepde*, not *mekdepda*; *Mara*, not *Marya*) or where the engine would cut; `readings(base)` lists a homograph's senses; `participle(verb, "yan" | "an" | "jak" | "maly", neg)`, `verbalNoun(verb, { poss, case })`, `voice(verb, "pass" | "refl" | "recip" | "caus")` (an infinitive: *ýazylmak*) and `negativeConverb(verb)` (*gelmän*) give the B1 non-finite forms; `noun`, `verb`, `attach(word, "(Y)m")`, `text` and `seg` are the building blocks. The B1 suffix ids for `build` and `analyze` are `YAN`, `DYK`, `MAK`, `SA`, `MAZDAN`, `YANCA`, `KA`, `CVB-A`, `YS`, `PASS`, `REFL`, `RECIP`, `CAUS`, `LA`, `LAS`, `LAN`, `AL`.

**Limits.** Written standard Turkmen only, A1 to B1 forms; no stress, no long vowels (they aren't written), no spoken forms (*gelenok, bilemok*). Since QA-TK-1 it builds `-kA` (*çagakam*, *gelýärkäm*, in the singular persons only). It doesn't build -(Y)w (*okuw, ýazuw*, lexical), the negative verbal noun -mAzlYk, vowel loss in a passive stem (*alnyp*) except the stems of `LEX.verbDrop` (*dogul-*, FC-4), or -sAdY. The particle after the ending in compound pasts (*bilýärdiňmi*) isn't one of its variants. A word missing from the lists follows the default rules. The analysis checks letters and boundaries, not meaning.

## 7. Content style rules

### 7.1 Turkmen orthography

- Standard written Turkmen in the Latin alphabet of 1993, as revised in 1999, spelled as in the official orthographic dictionary (*Türkmen diliniň orfografik sözlügi*) and explanatory dictionary (*Türkmen diliniň düşündirişli sözlügi*). When unsure about a spelling, check a dictionary rather than guessing, and log what was checked. No Cyrillic Turkmen.
- Only the 30 letters: a b ç d e ä f g h i j ž k l m n ň o ö p r s ş t u ü w y ý z. c, q, v and x only in foreign names written in their own spelling, and only in English text. The validator rejects ı, ğ, İ, â, î, û in Turkmen text.
- **y and ý are different letters**: *y* is a vowel (*gyz*, *ýyl*), *ý* a consonant (*ýok*, *aý*, *öý*). Check every word for both.
- **No apostrophe** before suffixes, on any word: *Aşgabatda*, *Merdanyň*, *2026-njy ýylda*, *BMG-niň*. Numerals take a hyphen before their suffix (*27-nji*, *1991-nji ýylda*, *5-e*).
- **Written attached**: the question particle -mY (*talypmy?*, *gelýärsiňmi?*, *dälmi?*); the locative-adjective -dAky (*öýdäki*); -ky (*meniňki*). **Written apart**: *bilen*, *üçin*, *ýaly*, *däl*, *hem*, *bar*, *ýok*, *eken*, the helper verbs (*okap otyr*, *gelip bilýärin*). Hyphenated: *ýa-da*, *şeýle-de bolsa*, reduplications (*ap-ak*, *ýuwaş-ýuwaş*). **Written as two words** (A1-1, from the explanatory dictionary on enedilim.com, reached through search): *şu gün* (in its examples under *bildiriş* and *aýak*), *hiç kim* (a sub-entry of *hiç*), *her bir* (in its examples under *ara*, *kese*, *watan*); *her kim* is written apart by the same pattern (not seen in a source).
- Day and month names in lowercase (*duşenbe*, *oktýabr*), also in full dates: *1991-nji ýylyň 27-nji oktýabry*.
- Sentences start with a capital letter and end with punctuation. Dialogue quotes in Turkmen text use “ ”. No apostrophe in Turkmen text at all (the validator warns on `'` and `’`).
- Text is NFC. ň is U+0148, ý U+00FD, ž U+017E, ä U+00E4.

### 7.2 Sounds the writing doesn't show

Guides and hints help the learner with what spelling hides, since there is no audio:

- Long vowels: say so when a word has one that matters (*at* name, *ot* fire, *üç*, *dört*, *ýaş*), and show the voicing it causes (*adym*).
- s and z as *th* sounds; ň as in *sing*; g and k with back vowels.
- e and ä: never interchangeable; *ä* is more open, like the a of *cat*.

When a guide gives a pronunciation, it uses plain English comparisons, never IPA alone.

### 7.3 Accepted answers

As turkish/PROJECT.md 7.3, with these Turkmen rules:

- **Pronouns.** With forms that carry a personal ending (-ýAr, -dY, -Ar, -sA, the copula with endings), accept the sentence with and without the pronoun. With the **future -jAk, the necessity -mAlY and the zero copula**, which carry no ending, the subject must be there: *Men ertir geljek* is right, *Ertir geljek* is wrong for "I will come tomorrow" (reject it with a why), unless the prompt is a question whose subject is already clear.
- **-ýAr and the progressive.** From a2-u11 on, accept the progressive with *otyr, dur, ýatyr, ýör* beside -ýAr for an English present continuous where both are natural.
- **-ýAr and -Ar.** For an English simple present describing a habit, accept both from a2-u04 on; for "will", accept -jAk and -Ar.
- **The question particle** written apart is never accepted; the checker makes it "almost" (6.4).
- **you**: *sen* and *siz* forms unless the prompt says "(singular)", "(plural)" or "(polite)", as in Turkish.
- **Check every list against its variants** (QA-TK-1). For each accepted entry, try the subject pronoun dropped or added, the genitive pronoun dropped or added before a possessed noun (*Meniň adym* / *Adym*), *sen* and *siz* with their possessives (*seniň … -(Y)ň*, *siziň … -(Y)ňYz*), and the time word before or after the subject (*Men ertir* / *Ertir men*); every gap QA-TK-1 found was one of these. **Wordbanks** need it most: a `wordbank` from English lists in `accept` the sentence without a pronoun the verb or the possessive already carries (*Düýn öýde ukladym*, *Bizi tanaýarlar*, *Adym Selbi*), since the learner may leave a tile unused.
- **QA-TK-2 on B1**: the B1 lists already had every variant above but a few, and these are the ones to check in new content: **a** beside **the** with a bare dative or locative (*bazara*: to the market, to a market; *kitaphanada*, *dellege*), the time word before the subject (*Öň sen…* beside *Sen öň…*), **her** beside **him** for *oňa* and *ony*, the *ol* form of the verb after *olar* (*Olar … at dakdy*), a subjectless `-Ar`, which carries its person (*Ertir geler*), and in a wordbank the sentence without the genitive pronoun of a relative or fact clause (*Maňa beren ýüzügiňi ýitirdim*, *Mara göçendigini bilmeýärdim*).
- **English *'d***: accept lists write it out as **had** or **would**; the checker tries both readings of a learner's *'d* (6.4), and the validator warns about *'d* in an accept entry.
- **Place names in English answers**: where the English spelling differs from the Turkmen one, accept both (*Dashoguz* and *Daşoguz*, *Merv* and *Merw*, *Turkmenbashi* and *Türkmenbaşy*), since the prompt shows the Turkmen one; the checker's typo step doesn't cover a short name (*Merw*) or two letters (*Turkmenbasy*).
- **The subjectless answer is a predictable mistake** with the zero copula, -jAk and -mAlY: list it in `reject` with the why (*Lukman.* → keep the subject: *Biz lukman*). Model text follows the same rule: a dialogue line, cloze text or mcq context keeps the subject of -jAk and -mAlY, except a general rule or sign (*Bu ýerde aýakgaby çykarmaly*) and a short answer whose subject is clear (*Ýok, gitmeli däl*).
- **English answers**: a bare Turkmen noun (no accusative, no possessive) can take **a** or **the** (*Kaka surata seredýär*: at the picture, at a picture); -ýAr takes the simple present beside the continuous unless a word such as *häzir* decides (*Kaka Aşgabada gidýär*: Dad goes, Dad is going).
- Everything else (articles, word order, spellings of English, digits, full forms) as in Turkish.

### 7.8 Flashcards

As turkish/PROJECT.md 7.8 (one card per lemma, examples ≤ 10 words in A1–A2 grammar, up to four glosses, notes for false friends and irregularities), except for the source of the order.

**The word list.** `scripts/turkmen/tm-wordlist.tsv` holds the 1,000 lemmas in deck order: UTF-8, LF line endings, a header row and one tab-separated row per rank with `rank`, `lemma`, `pos`, `sense` (empty or 1, 2), `gloss` (short, for reviewers) and `tr` (the Turkish lemma it was matched to, or `+` for an added word). The card of rank 42 is `fc-0042` and takes its `tk`, `pos` and `sense` from row 42; the validator checks every card against its row. FC-2 to FC-5 write ranks 201–1000 in this order. A wrong row (lemma, spelling, part of speech) is fixed with its card and logged; IDs never change.

**Source and method (TK-PLAN).** No Turkmen word-frequency list was reachable from the session that made the deck (the Leipzig Corpora Collection and Wikipedia dumps were blocked, and FrequencyWords has no Turkmen list), so the order is **adapted from the Daily Turkish deck**: each of the Turkish course's 1,000 ranked lemmas (`scripts/turkish/wordlist.tsv`, itself adapted from the 2018 Turkish list of FrequencyWords by Hermit Dave, CC BY-SA 4.0, built from OpenSubtitles2018) was matched by meaning to its usual Turkmen equivalent. Then, by hand: words of film dialogue that a Turkmen learner rarely needs were dropped or moved down (crime and dubbing words), several Turkish lemmas that share one Turkmen word were merged, Turkmen words with no Turkish counterpart in the list were added where their everyday frequency would put them (*oglan, haçan, çörek, oba, çöl, düýe, haly, telpek*, days of the week…), and a few were moved up for learners (*türkmen, dil, çaý, mugallym, talyp*, the colours). **Ranks are an editorial estimate, not a Turkmen corpus count**, and every `source` says so. Because the order derives from a CC BY-SA list, `tm-wordlist.tsv` is shared under CC BY-SA 4.0 with that credit (LICENSE.txt gets a line in TK-PUBLISH). Glosses, examples and notes are our own. If a Turkmen corpus becomes reachable later, a conversation may re-rank the list; card IDs stay.

**Part of speech decisions:** *bar* and *ýok* are adj (as in the Turkish deck); *ýok* also carries "no" as its second gloss; *däl* and *-mY* are particles (the card's `tk` is `-my`); quantifiers (*her, ähli, käbir, birnäçe*) are det; *bolýar* "OK" and *salam* are interj; phrase cards for fixed formulas (*sag bol, haýyş edýärin, hoş gal, hoş geldiňiz*); compound verbs as one lemma (*pikir etmek, dowam etmek, gowy görmek, satyn almak, jaň etmek, ýatdan çykarmak*); noun + *etmek* goes to the noun card when the noun stands alone (*kömek*).

**`unit` and `level`**, and the vocab links (`card`), follow turkish/PROJECT.md 7.8 ("unit, level and vocab links") unchanged: content batches set `unit` on the cards their lessons teach first and `card` on their vocab entries. QA-TK-1 checked all 600 cards against the A1–A2 lessons by script: every card a lesson headword teaches has its `unit`, `level` and link, except homographs met in another sense (*daş* stone against *daş* outside, the noun *ýüz* face against the number); *onda* (fc-0108) gets a1-u05, the vocab entry of a1-u05-l03 being its sense "on him, with him"; *ot* (fire, fc-0346) has no unit, since only a table of the a1-u01 guide shows it and `gp-vowel-length` names *at* alone (*at*, horse, is named there and drilled in a1-u01-l03). Taught by a form or a compound, as in Turkish: *bagyşlamak* (*bagyşlaň*), *kömek* and *tans* (with *etmek*), *bolýar* (*Bolýarmy?*), *bolmak* (*bolmaz*). The validator warns when a vocab entry links a card that has no `unit` or a later one (8).

Same errors and warnings as turkish/PROJECT.md 8, with the Turkmen letter rules of 7.1 in place of the Turkish ones: errors for the Turkish-only letters (ı, ğ, İ, â, î, û), `listen` exercises, the word "Turkish" in lesson, guide, interlude or card text (a warning, so a writer checks that it is a brief factual remark in a guide or interlude, not a bridge from Turkish), a malformed `scripts/turkmen/tm-wordlist.tsv` or a card that differs from its row; warnings for a question particle written apart (*talyp my*), an apostrophe in Turkmen text, *y*/*ý* patterns that look wrong (a *ý* between two consonants, a *y* starting a word before a vowel), a future or necessitative sentence with no subject in an `accept[0]`, a vocab `card` link to a card with no `unit` or with a later `unit` than the lesson's (QA-TK-1); markup in a plain-text field, *'d* in an English accept entry, a link in an English option (QA-TK-2, ported from Daily Turkish's QA-2; options are checked by `optionsLang`, 5.0). **Wrong options are exempt** (A1-1): spelling drills show misspellings on purpose (*yok*, *hava*, *neme*), so the wrong options of an `mcq` or of a cloze blank with options, and the distractors of a `wordbank`, are checked only for the Turkish-only letters (errors); the right option, the tiles of the answer and everything else get every check.

**The morphology engine** (section 3), as in turkish/PROJECT.md 8: for each `conjugate` exercise whose `form` it knows, the validator builds every cell's form from `base` and checks that the cell accepts it, and that everything the cell accepts is a form the engine builds (one of its variants, the ol form for olar, or a variant with words in front, as in *Men talyp*); a `base` with two readings (*at*) is checked with the reading that fits. For each `morph` exercise it checks that the tiles are `base` and its suffixes, cut at morpheme boundaries; if not, the warning gives the word the same suffixes build (*mekdepde*, not *mekdepda*) or the engine's cut (*öý\|ümiz*). For each flashcard it checks `forms` (`prs`, `past`, `aor`, `cvb`, `fut`, `acc`, `poss3`, `pl`) against the third-person forms the engine builds. A `form` slug it doesn't know, or tiles it can't analyse, are listed as not checked above the summary line, which counts what was checked. These are warnings, as in Turkish, while the engine meets new content. The Turkish spec planned to make them errors once a few batches had passed with none; QA-TK-2 checked that for Turkmen and kept them warnings: run with the engine QA-TK-1 left (commit 62c6b65), the content written since gives 27 engine warnings (B1-5's causatives and FC-4 and FC-5's cards: the soft *t* of *okatmak*, *döretmek*, *düzetmek*; *maksady*, *sowgady*, *deňzi*; *doglar*, *doglup*) and one morph exercise the engine couldn't analyse (*az\|al\|t\|dy*), each settled by correcting the engine, not the content. `--strict`, which every batch runs, already turns them into errors. The next conversation to meet them is TK-REVIEW (apply); if the native speaker's corrections pass with no engine warning, a later conversation can make `engineConjugate`, `engineMorph` and `engineCard` report errors. `morph-test.mjs` runs over 2,100 forms typed by hand: run it after any change to the engine. `--examples` validates the `json lesson`, `json block`, `json flashcards` and `json progress` examples of this file.

**Native review.** Turkmen is written here with AI assistance and checked against dictionaries where they can be reached; that is not enough to call it finished. After each level, a TK-REVIEW conversation exports the level's Turkmen text (sentences, accept lists, guide examples, card examples) into a review sheet for a native speaker, and a second TK-REVIEW conversation applies the corrections. The sheet is made by `node scripts/turkmen/review-export.mjs <level>` (scripts/turkmen/README.md): `turkmen/review/<level>-review.xlsx`, one row per distinct Turkmen string with its English, what to check, the reviewer's columns **OK?**, **Correction** and **Comment**, and its locations (file and JSON path); the uncertain forms of the progress log and the open questions of TM-CURRICULUM.md come first. `<level>-review-note.md` is the note for the reviewer in English and Turkmen, also the sheet's first tab. The export changes no content; rerun it after any change to the level. The beta notice stays until a level is reviewed. The log records who reviewed what (initials only). Definition of done for a content batch: as in Turkish (every unit complete, `--batch` reports it complete, zero errors, `index.json` regenerated, files re-read, log updated), plus the list of uncertain forms in the log row. Nothing is committed or pushed: Grégoire reviews the changes and pushes them to `main`, and pushing publishes them at dailyturkish.app/turkmen/, so a batch that changes what learners see is checked in Chromium at 360 px before the conversation ends.
